How it stays honest
Everything here is real NEON data, saved ahead of time and bundled with the app. Every number tells you how many measurements it is based on, so you can see when a result rests on plenty of data and when it rests on only a little. When the app draws a line between two things, it shows how well the line fits and warns when readings taken close together in time can make a link look stronger than it is. When it checks whether two things move together, it uses a method that holds up even when the numbers are lopsided, treats anything based on fewer than 8 measurements as shaky, and reminds you that checking many things at once turns up some matches by luck. The seasonal pattern is pulled straight from the real record, not a smooth curve made up to look nice. Amounts too low for the instruments to measure are marked, not hidden. Each thing in the water uses a reviewed unit target; registered legacy unit anomalies are excluded rather than silently converted (and bromide is not bicarbonate, chloride is not chlorine).
